# Peloton Chronicle v1.0 — final deterministic narrative benchmark

The final balance pass used a fixed eight-season universe (`seed 1301`) after the v0.9 structural tests.

## Era-defining careers observed

- Alejandro Rodríguez — Generational, Grand Tour specialist: **6 Grand Tours and 30 stages in eight seasons**. He changed teams four times, showing that a dominant talent can still have an unstable career context. At a normal 11–14 season career pace, the engine now permits the requested 7–10 Grand Tour historical ceiling without scripting it.
- Lars Smit — Generational, Monument specialist: **7 Monuments, 13 other classics and 20 total races in eight seasons**. This projects naturally toward the 10 Monument / 20–22 classics range in a full elite career.
- Eli Anderson and Wout De Clercq — Legends, Grand Tour specialists: **4 and 3 Grand Tours**, creating credible secondary dynasties and direct era rivals.

## Alternative and disappointing careers observed

- A Generational one-week specialist won only one Grand Tour but accumulated major stage success.
- Multiple Generational riders finished the benchmark with one or no elite race wins, including riders whose careers ended early or whose team changes did not unlock their potential.
- Epic and Rare specialists still won major one-day races, while only one Rare rider reached a single Grand Tour victory after the final GC quality adjustment.

## Final tuning decisions

- No hard caps were added to Grand Tours, Monuments or classics.
- Generational riders receive slightly longer career opportunities and a modest repeat-major experience effect.
- Team quality, director quality, facilities, targeting, fatigue and annual shape remain capable of interrupting an elite rider's peak.
- Grand Tour classification now penalizes insufficient sustained quality more strongly than individual stages, preserving breakaway surprises while preventing low-level riders from repeatedly winning three-week races.
- Classics remain much more open than Grand Tours; Rare and occasional Uncommon riders can still produce historic one-day upsets.

These are deterministic distribution checks, not guaranteed scripts. Other seeds can produce weaker eras, two-way rivalries, failed prodigies, dominant teams, or fragmented championships.
