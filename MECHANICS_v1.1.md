# Peloton Chronicle v1.1 — Market, sponsorship and team-building design

## 1. Elite population and generation

The active universe maintains fixed headline pools:

| Rarity | Active target | Replacement rule |
|---|---:|---|
| Generational | 3 | One U23 prospect appears only after one retires |
| Legend | 9 | One U23 prospect appears only after one retires |
| Epic | 18 | One U23 prospect appears only after one retires |

All other riders are procedurally generated as Rare, Uncommon or Common. Existing saves with excess elite riders are normalized during migration. Opening elite riders are distributed primarily through WorldTour teams; lower-tier elite riders are young development cases rather than mature stars stranded in Continental cycling.

A rider becomes a mandatory WorldTour scouting target when potential is at least 90 and one of these applies:

- Age 23 or older
- Career multiplier is at least 0.90
- Current ability is at least 84

WorldTour teams make room for those riders using a combined present-ability and potential score. Deterministic QA verifies that no mature 90+ rider remains below WorldTour after an offseason.

## 2. Rider happiness and contracts

Happiness combines:

- Salary versus expected market salary
- Team results relative to tier and budget
- Facilities
- Director ability
- Sporting role
- Sponsor security
- Elite riders being held below WorldTour

A rider can enter the market because a contract expires, happiness becomes critically low, the salary is severely below market, or an elite rider is ready for WorldTour competition.

WorldTour teams prioritize renewing established elite riders. A WorldTour elite rider cannot be signed downward merely to fill a ProSeries roster. Direct elite poaching is capped each offseason so Le Grand Braquet contains a few important moves rather than dozens of arbitrary changes.

## 3. Sponsor database and identity

Sponsors are independent entities with:

- Country
- Size: Huge, Large, Medium or Small
- Financial strength
- Attraction
- Two brand colors

Each team has:

- **Primary sponsor:** provides most income, controls the public team name and supplies the dominant color
- **Secondary sponsor:** adds income and attraction and appears in the maillot identity
- **Permanent organization identity:** survives sponsor changes

Eligibility follows sponsor scale:

- Huge and Large sponsors can lead WorldTour teams
- Medium sponsors can lead ProSeries and lower projects
- Small sponsors are normally primary sponsors only in Continental/U23, or secondary sponsors below WorldTour

The database mixes current, historical and plausible future brands. Sponsor contracts last multiple seasons and can renew or be replaced based on results, national fit and financial health.

## 4. Team finances

Annual income consists of:

1. Primary sponsor income
2. Secondary sponsor income
3. Prize money generated by sporting results

Annual expenses consist of:

1. Rider payroll
2. Race-director salary
3. Facilities investment made that offseason

The team page shows each component and projected balance. Budget and attraction are recalculated from sponsor strength, secondary support and organization reputation.

## 5. Facilities

Facilities use a continuous 1–10 scale. They influence rider performance, development environment and recruitment appeal.

Facilities decay gradually. Teams choose whether to invest according to resources, ambition and underperformance. Upgrade cost is nonlinear: improving an ordinary setup is affordable, while moving from an already elite facility toward 10 becomes disproportionately expensive.

Examples of intended behavior:

- 6.0 → 6.5: routine investment
- 8.8 → 9.3: major investment
- 9.5 → 10.0: exceptional strategic commitment

## 6. Race directors

Every team has one race director. Agencies hold unemployed directors.

Director value combines:

- Overall ability
- Tactics
- Rider development
- Scouting
- Recruitment

Director happiness combines salary, results, resources and pressure. Teams review directors in the offseason. High-budget WorldTour teams react most strongly to missing Grand Tours, Monuments or points expectations.

A director may be:

- Renewed
- Dismissed to an agency
- Hired from an agency
- Poached from another team
- Retired and procedurally replaced

A director cannot move again until two full seasons have passed since the previous appointment or dismissal. Elite directors in lower tiers are visible to richer WorldTour projects, but sporting fit and salary affordability still matter.

## 7. Promotion and relegation

The universe preserves exactly 18 WorldTour teams and 16 ProSeries teams. Sporting cycles create:

- WorldTour ↔ ProSeries swaps
- ProSeries ↔ Continental swaps
- Occasional new projects and organizational closures

A tier change updates the team, riders, roster limits, finances and historical record. Le Grand Braquet lists each preseason change explicitly.

## 8. Preseason information architecture

Le Grand Braquet’s preseason section contains curated blocks:

1. Season odds
2. Generational/Legend/Epic signings only
3. New elite prospects
4. Elite retirements
5. Director appointments
6. Sponsor changes
7. Promotion and relegation

The complete market remains available on the Market screen, but the magazine presents only events that define the coming season.

## 9. UX and navigation rules

- Rider cards show `Y current / career length`.
- Team cards use sponsor colors and show business/sporting metrics.
- Team lists support tier filtering and sorting by points, budget, facilities, attraction, reputation or director ability.
- Rider lists support tier, rarity, race-program and terrain filters.
- Detail pages use a navigation stack, so Back returns to the team, magazine, ranking or list that opened the entity.
- Race targets use explicit statuses instead of the ambiguous “Did not place.”

## 10. Save migration

Version 8 migration adds missing sponsor contracts, finances, facilities, salaries, director contracts, happiness and elite-pool normalization to older saves. Historical results remain untouched. New universes are recommended when evaluating opening-population balance because existing riders retain their prior ages, teams and careers.
